EGUSI SOUP

Egusi Soup is a highly celebrated, rich West African delicacy made from the ground seeds of protein-dense melon varieties. Often considered a staple comfort food in Nigeria, this thick and hearty soup features a unique, nutty texture combined with palm oil, leafy greens, assorted meats, and seafood. It is traditionally enjoyed with "swallow" sides like pounded yam, fufu, or eba

This particular recipe is for servings of 8


Recipe Overview

  • Prep time:15 to 20 minutes
  • Cook time:60 to 75 minutes

Ingredients

The base Protein & Stock

  • 1 kg (2.2 lbs) Assorted meats (combination of beef, goat meat, shaki, or cow leg), cut into bite-sized chunks.

  • 1 medium Onion, chopped (divided in half for boiling and frying).

  • 2-3 Bouillon cubes (Maggi or Knorr).

  • 1 teaspoon Salt

  • 4-5 cups Water

    The Soup Base:

  • 2 cups Ground Egusi (melon seeds)

  • 1 cup Red Palm Oil.

  • 3-4 Scotch bonnet peppers

  • 2 Red bell peppers

  • 3 tablespoons Ground crayfish.

  • 1-2 cups Smoked fish or stockfish

  • 2 tablespoons Fermented locust beans (Iru) – optional

    • The Greens

  • 2-3 cups Fresh spinach or Ugu leaves (fluted pumpkin leaves), washed and chopped.

Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Prep and Cook the Meat

  1. In a large pot, combine your assorted meats, half of the chopped onion, 2 bouillon cubes, and a teaspoon of salt.

  2. Pour in just enough water to cover the meat. Cover and cook over medium heat for about 30 to 45 minutes until the meat is completely tender.

  3. Separate the meat from the broth and save the stock—this is where your soup gets its rich flavor.

Step 2: Make the Egusi paste

  1. While the meat cooks, put the ground egusi powder into a small bowl.

  2. Add a little warm water or a ladle of your meat stock (about ½ cup) and mix thoroughly until it forms a thick, moldable paste. Set aside.

Step 3: Fry the Base

  1. Wipe your main cooking pot clean, then heat the palm oil on medium heat for about 1–2 minutes (do not let it smoke).

  2. Add the remaining chopped onion and the locust beans (iru). Fry for 3–5 minutes until fragrant.

  3. Pour in the coarse-blended scotch bonnet and red bell peppers. Fry this mixture for about 8 to 10 minutes until the water evaporates and the oil begins to separate.

Step 4: Cook the Egusi Lumps

  1. Reduce the heat to medium-low. Scoop small spoonfuls of the egusi paste gently into the frying pepper base.

  2. Do not stir. Cover the pot and let the paste steam and fry in the oil undisturbed for about 10 minutes. This allows the egusi to cake and form nice, firm lumps.

  3. After 10 minutes, gently flip or stir the egusi chunks so they cook evenly for another 5 minutes.

Step 5: Bring it Together

  1. Pour in your reserved meat stock, the cooked meats, deboned smoked fish, and the ground crayfish.

  2. Stir gently so you don't completely break up the egusi lumps. If the soup looks too thick, add a splash of water.

  3. Taste the liquid and adjust with the remaining bouillon cube or extra salt if needed. Simmer on low heat for 15 minutes so the flavors meld.

Step 6: Add the Vegetables

  1. Fold in your chopped spinach or Ugu leaves.

  2. Stir well and leave to simmer uncovered for an additional 3 to 5 minutes so the leaves stay vibrated and bright green.

  3. Turn off the heat and let the soup rest before serving.
