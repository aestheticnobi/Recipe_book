JELLOF RICE

Jollof rice is a beloved, one-pot seasoned rice dish that originated in West Africa, tracing its roots back to the Wolof Empire in the Senegambian region. Renowned for its vibrant orange-red color and bold flavors.

This particular recipe is for servings of 8


Recipe Overview

  • Prep time:15 minutes
  • Cook time:45 minutes

Ingredients

  • The tomato/pepper bas
    • 4 large tomatoes
    • 3 large red bell peppers
    • 2 scotch bonnet pepper
    • 1 medium red onion

  • The Rice & Aromatics
    • 4 cups long-grain parboiled rice
    • ½ cup vegetable oil
    • 1 medium red onion, finely sliced
    • 5 tablespoons tomato paste
    • 4 cups rich chicken stock
    • 3 bay leaves (optional)
    • 1 tablespoon curry powder
    • 1 tablespoon dried thyme
    • 2 Knorr / Maggi bouillon cubes
    • Salt
    • 2 tablespoons butter (optional)
    • 1 medium tomato & 1 small onion, sliced (optional)

Step-Step Instructions

Step 1: Blend and Reduce the Base

  1. Rough chop the tomatoes, bell peppers, Scotch bonnets, and 1 onion.

  2. Blend them together until completely smooth.

  3. Pour the blended mixture into a pot and boil it on medium-high heat for about 10–12 minutes until some of the water evaporates and it thickens into a paste-like consistency. Set aside.

Step 2: Fry the tomato

  1. Heat the vegetable oil in a large, sturdy pot over medium heat. Add the sliced onion and bay leaves, frying for 3–4 minutes until the onions are soft and fragrant.

  2. Stir in the tomato paste. Fry this continuously for 5–7 minutes on medium-low heat, stirring constantly so it doesn't burn. It should turn a darker crimson color and look slightly grainy; this removes the sour tin taste.

  3. Pour in your reduced pepper blend. Add the curry powder, dried thyme, crushed bouillon cubes, and a pinch of salt.
  4. Mix thoroughly, cover, and let it fry for 10–15 minutes on medium heat. You will know it is ready when the oil separates from the sauce and floats to the top.

Step 3: Combine and Steam

  1. While the sauce fries, thoroughly wash your parboiled rice in warm water multiple times to rid it of excess starch until the water runs clear. Drain it fully in a sieve.

  2. Pour the chicken stock into the fried tomato sauce and bring it to a rolling boil. Taste the liquid—it should taste slightly over-seasoned because the rice will absorb a lot of the flavor. Adjust with salt if necessary.

  3. Add the washed rice to the pot and stir gently to coat every grain in the sauce. The liquid level should be just barely touching or slightly above the top of the rice.

  4. The Lock-In: Immediately place a sheet of aluminum foil or a clean plastic bag over the pot, then press the lid down tightly over it to trap the steam. Turn the heat down to the lowest setting possible.

  5. Let it cook undisturbed for 25–30 minutes.

Step 4: The Final Touch & "Party" Smoke

  1. Open the pot, add the optional sliced tomato, small onion, and butter on top.

  2. Gently fluff the rice from the bottom up with a fork or wooden spatula to distribute the sauce evenly.

  3. For the Party Flavor: If you want that authentic smoky flavor, turn the heat up to medium-high for the last 3 minutes without stirring. Let the bottom layer of rice intentionally burn and crackle. Turn off the heat, keep it covered for 5 minutes to let the smoke infuse, then give it a final fluff and serve!
